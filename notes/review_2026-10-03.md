# Technical Review: Alameda County Property Tax Imbalance Assessment

**Reviewer:** senior analytics professional (IC6 data science), frontier tech. Blunt by design.
**Date:** October 3, 2026
**Status of findings:** Finding #1 independently re-verified by Neboleon against the tax rolls (Oct 3, 2026) — confirmed, pattern even cleaner than reported. Finding #2 verified on logic; decomposition per reviewer.

---

## (a) Verdict

The core identification strategy is sound and the data work is careful — the paired 2023 test and the parcel-level Prop 8 trajectory verification are genuinely good analysis, and the honest scrapping of the contaminated v1 builds credibility. **But the single most load-bearing number in the project — the +$52k/+4.4% peak-buyer over-assessment headline — is overstated by roughly a third because the analysis applies a uniform 1.02³ Prop 13 trajectory that the county's own rolls refute**, and the "27% received Prop 8 relief" claim sweeps in transfer-driven declines that aren't Prop 8 at all. Fix the trajectory factor, redefine the Prop 8 detection window, recompute — the qualitative story survives, but several published numbers need to move before this goes public.

## (b) Strengths

1. **Killing v1 was the right call, and documenting it was the strongest move in the project.** The refinance contamination was large ($822k vs $1.22M medians) and the correction note in `methodology_gold_standard.md` is honest about both-side contamination. Most analysts would have quietly revised; this one wrote it up.
2. **The paired 2023 test is well-identified.** True purchase prices on both sides, the correct counterfactual (prior 2% trajectory, not the mechanical post-sale reassessment), and a clean result (median ratio ≈ 1.00 after correction — the machinery works as designed).
3. **Prop 8 verification from actual roll trajectories is rare and strong.** Year-over-year assessed-value paths from seven tax rolls beat inferring relief from cross-sections. The 2026-cycle wave (~1,000 fresh cuts) is a genuine empirical finding.
4. **Careful, honestly-documented data work.** The 2025 TRA-layer truncation, the zero-padded join keys (94% miss rate on naive join), the transfer-list dedup — all real gotchas, all caught and disclosed.
5. **Triangulation.** The paired test's 22.2% below-purchase-price and the corrected trajectory-union 21.7% are two independent measures converging — that's what robustness looks like.
6. **The ZIP note separates proven from interpreted**, which is exactly the discipline this kind of project needs.

## (c) Weaknesses, ordered by severity

### 1. CRITICAL — The uniform 1.02³ trajectory factor is empirically wrong [VERIFIED]
**Files:** `src/cohort_gaps_v3.py` (paired expected value; cross-section factored values); `src/charts_final.py` (dashed 2% line); project page "Peak buyers" section; essay "Peak buyers are over-assessed"; `notes/why_varies_by_zip.md` ("gap ≈ 6%").

**Finding:** Joining 2023 buyers' transfer prices to their first post-purchase roll (`2024_to_2025`, lien Jan 1, 2024), the ratio AV/price is **bimodal by transfer month** (re-verified Oct 3, 2026):

| Transfer month (2023) | n | Median AV/price | Share ≈1.00 | Share ≈1.02 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr | 551 | 1.020 | 4% | 87% |
| May | 689 | 1.020 | 2% | 88% |
| Jun | 593 | 1.020 | 3% | 88% |
| Jul | 605 | 1.000 | 88% | 0% |
| Aug | 639 | 1.000 | 88% | 0% |
| Sep | 550 | 1.000 | 91% | 0% |
| Oct | 584 | 1.000 | 96% | 0% |
| Nov | 502 | 1.000 | 96% | 0% |
| Dec | 410 | 1.000 | 95% | 0% |

The mechanism is almost certainly the assessment-roll close (~June 30): H1 transfers get the earlier base lien date (factor applies), H2 transfers don't. Whatever the statute says, **the county's practice as measured is: no inflation factor at the first lien date after purchase for H2 buyers.**

**Impact:** "Assessed today" = lien Jan 2026. Correct factor: 1.02³ for H1-2023 buyers, **1.02² for H2-2023 buyers** (the majority). The analysis applies 1.02³ to everyone, overstating expected AV by ~2% for ~55–60% of the sample:
- Paired test median ratio 0.98 → **~1.00 corrected** (strengthens "machinery works"; the 22% below-price is unaffected since it uses no factor).
- Cross-section gap +$52k (+4.4%) → **~+$38k (~+3.2%)**, and the bootstrap CI [+$19k, +$73k] shifts to roughly **[+$5k, +$59k]** — uncomfortably close to zero.
- The trajectory chart's dashed 2% line is drawn as if the factor accrues from purchase for everyone; the blue cohort median sits ~2–4 points below it at every post-purchase x, **visually implying under-trajectory when the cohort is in fact exactly on its true trajectory**.
- "Approximately 6% (three years of factored growth)" in the ZIP decomposition → ~4–5% for the typical buyer.

### 2. HIGH — The "27% Prop 8" detection window includes non-Prop-8 declines
**Files:** `src/trajectories.py` (YoY-decline detector); project page; essay.

**Finding:** For a 2023 buyer, the `2023_to_2024` roll (lien Jan 1, 2023) **predates the purchase** — it's the seller's assessed value. A "cut" in the 2023→2024 transition means the buyer paid less than the seller's old AV: a transfer-driven reassessment, not Prop 8. Pre-2023 windows reflect the *seller's* relief, not the buyer's. Decomposing parcels with any roll-window cut:
- 46 had cuts **only** in pre-purchase windows (seller's relief)
- 33 had cuts **only** in the 2023→2024 transfer window (bought below seller's AV)
- Only the 2024→2025-roll transition is strictly post-purchase: **95 parcels (1.9%)**

**Corrected headline:** 95 (roll window) + 1,029 (2026 cycle, resales excluded) − 13 overlap = **1,111 of 5,113 = 21.7%**, median clean cut depth **8.6%** (not 9.8%). Note the 27.2% was computed in an ephemeral heredoc — **it exists in no output file and cannot be reproduced from the repo** (`outputs/trajectories.json` on disk still says 8.4%).

The qualitative finding actually gets *more* interesting stated correctly: only 1.9% had relief through the 2025 roll; 20% got it in the 2026 cycle. "Years late" is the story, not "concentrated in 2024."

### 3. HIGH — The cross-section gap is a difference of medians of *different homes*
**File:** `src/cohort_gaps_v3.py`.

`gap = med(2023 prices × 1.02³) − med(2024–25 prices)`: the 5,124 buyers and 7,909 sellers are different parcels. Composition shift between the seller pools flows straight into the gap. The bootstrap (1,000 reps, independent resampling of each side) quantifies **sampling noise of medians only** — it ignores (a) within-ZIP clustering (CI too narrow — needs ZIP-block bootstrap or disclosure), (b) composition shift, (c) timing mismatch. Presenting "95% CI: +$19k to +$73k" as the uncertainty of "over-assessment" overstates what it covers.

Related overclaim: **"+$52,000 (+4.4%) above today's market prices"** — B is 2024–25 sale prices, not today's prices. Honest statement: roughly "+$38k (~+3%) above 2024–25 sale prices, sampling uncertainty only, composition risk unquantified" (after finding #1's correction).

### 4. MEDIUM — The ZIP "mirroring" is near-tautological
`gap% = med23×1.0612/med2425 − 1` and `price change = med2425/med23 − 1` are algebraically linked: gap% ≈ 6.12% − chg to first order. The ranking correspondence is **mechanical, not independent confirmation** — same transfer medians rearranged. The genuine empirical content is (a) the price divergence itself (real, independently corroborated by ZHVI — good), and (b) the pooled gap level. Words like "exactly" and "unmistakable" oversell. Per-ZIP gaps rest on small samples (94705: n23=30) with **no uncertainty shown**.

The transfer-based ZIP price changes are median-vs-median across different homes (same composition caveat as #3). Lean on ZHVI for the divergence claim; present transfer medians as supporting.

### 5. MEDIUM — Effective-rate market proxy biases the 4.6x upward; chart has an unexplained break
Market value = ZIP×type **median** 2024–25 transfer price applied to every parcel. Longtime owners plausibly own below-median-value homes → denominator too large → their rate understated → 4.6x overstated. Sensitivity: if pre-1990 homes are worth ~20% less than ZIP median, true rate ~0.25% not 0.20%, gradient compresses to ~3.5–4x. Still large — do the ±20% sensitivity and show it.

Separately, the published chart **shows the 2026 bar at 0.70%** with no explanation — the 2026 cohort (partial year) is dominated by non-reset documents; the document-year proxy degrades at the right edge. Drop 2026 (with footnote) or explain it. Also: text names **2022 (0.93%)** as top cohort while data max is **2024 (0.944%)**.

### 6. MEDIUM — Tenure gradient: "produced entirely by the 2%-per-year cap"
Assessed-value medians by document-year bucket don't control for home size/quality. (The effective-rate result, which normalizes by market value and shows an even *steeper* gradient, argues against size-confounding — say that explicitly instead of "entirely.") Also: refinance pollution in recent buckets carries old low AVs, biasing the 3.5x **downward** — the gradient is conservative, which strengthens the case and costs one sentence.

### 7. LOW — v1 scripts aren't labeled superseded *in code*
`src/cohort_gaps.py`'s docstring reads as a live analysis. The "kept for transparency" framing exists only in notes. One-line fix: `SUPERSEDED — do not cite` banner in v1/v2 docstrings.

### 8. LOW — Assorted
- **`value_from_trans_tax` inversion unverified.** Several Alameda cities levy their own transfer taxes atop the county $1.10/$1,000. If the county inverts using the county rate only, Oakland/Berkeley/Piedmont prices are overstated. Medians look sane and paired ratios ≈1.0, so risk is low — but run one check: `assessed/(price×factor)` distribution by city.
- **2025 partial-year** in sample B — disclosed once; also affects seasonal mix. Minor.
- **ZHVI smoothing lag.** File is `_sm_sa`; trailing smoother lags turning points 1–3 months; covers SFR+condos middle-tier while analysis is SFR. "Spring 2022" is coarse enough to survive; assert Apr–Jun or soften to "2022."
- **94612** (downtown Oakland, largely PO boxes) in the 47-ZIP counts with −36% "drawdown." Exclude non-residential ZIPs from headline counts.
- **HOEX noise in Prop 8 detection:** `Total_Net_Value` is net of exemptions; a new $7,000 homeowner's exemption reads as a "cut." Checked: only 1 of 1,029 2026-cycle cuts in the $5.5k–$8.5k band — negligible. But detector should compare gross values or assert on exemption fields.
- **Paired-test resale contamination:** 27 of 1,161 below-price parcels resold in 2024–25 (2.3%) — negligible, but make scripts consistent (trajectory script excludes resales; paired script doesn't).

## (d) Concrete fixes

1. **Recompute with buyer-specific trajectory factors** (≤Jun 2023 → 1.02³; ≥Jul 2023 → 1.02²; or use each parcel's observed 2024-roll factor). Republish: paired median ratio, cross-section gap + CI, per-ZIP gaps, "6%" decomposition (→ ~4–5%). Expect: paired 0.98→~1.00; gap +$52k→~+$38k; CI → ~[+$5k, +$59k].
2. **Redefine Prop 8 detection as strictly post-purchase declines**; republish as **~22% (1,111/5,113), median depth 8.6%**; fix "concentrated in 2024/2025" → 1.9% through 2025 roll, ~20% wave in 2026 cycle. **Commit the computation as a script** — the headline must be reproducible from the repo.
3. **Reword the gap claim**: "~+$38k (~+3%) above 2024–25 sale prices (bootstrap 95% CI ≈ [+$5k, +$59k]; sampling uncertainty only)." Never "today's market prices."
4. **ZIP section**: state mirroring as mechanical; show n's with per-ZIP gaps; caveat n≈30 ZIPs; lean on ZHVI for divergence.
5. **Effective rates**: ±20% sensitivity band or frank directional caveat; drop/explain 2026 bar; cite 2024 (0.94%) as max cohort.
6. **Chart fixes**: realign trajectory chart's 2% line to cohort purchase timing; SUPERSEDED banners on v1/v2 docstrings.
7. **Tenure gradient**: replace "entirely" with size caveat + conservative-bias note.
8. **Verify transfer-tax inversion** with city-level paired-ratio check; exclude 94612-type ZIPs from headline counts.

## (e) Claims that are flat-out wrong (must change before publication)

1. **"27% have received a Prop 8 decline-in-value reduction… concentrated in the 2024 and 2025 rolls"** — wrong number (21.7% on correct definition) and wrong mechanism (2024 "concentration" is transfer-driven).
2. **"+$52,000 (+4.4%) above today's market prices"** — wrong level (~+$38k/~+3%) and wrong label (2024–25 sale prices).
3. **"Buyers from the 2021–22 price peak sit a median 4.4% above market"** — estimate is for **2023** buyers; the 2021–22 cohort was never cleanly estimated.
4. **Trajectory chart's dashed "Prop 13 2% factored growth (from each purchase)" line** — misaligned with cohort purchase timing.
5. **"the over-assessment ranking mirrors the price drop ranking exactly"** — "exactly" claims discovery about an algebraic identity.

None of these sinks the project. The two-sided distortion is real, the Prop 8 verification is real, and the ZIP divergence is real. But the current draft publishes at least three numbers that don't survive contact with the project's own data — fix them, and this is a strong piece of public analysis.
